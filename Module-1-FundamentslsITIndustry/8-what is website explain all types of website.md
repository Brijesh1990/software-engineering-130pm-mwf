# What Is a Website?

A **website** is a collection of related web pages and other resources, such as images, videos, and documents, that are usually available under one domain name. People access websites over a network using a web browser and a URL.

For example, a website might have a home page, an About page, a contact page, and several articles. A **webpage** is one individual page; a **website** is the collection of pages and resources.

## How a Website Works

1. A person enters a URL, follows a link, or selects a search result in a browser.
2. The browser requests the website's content from a web server.
3. The server returns a page or data. Some sites also run application code or retrieve information from a database.
4. The browser displays the content and may run scripts that make the page interactive.

Websites commonly use HTML for page structure, CSS for presentation, and JavaScript for interactive behavior, but many other technologies can be involved.

## Common Types of Websites by Purpose

Website types can overlap: one site may be both a business website and an online store, for example.

### 1. Personal Website

A personal website shares information about an individual, their interests, projects, or experiences. It may include a biography, photos, or links to other work.

### 2. Portfolio Website

A portfolio presents a person's or organization's work, skills, and accomplishments. Artists, designers, photographers, and developers often use portfolios to show examples of their work.

### 3. Blog

A blog publishes posts, usually shown in date order and often grouped by topic. A blog may be personal, educational, professional, or part of a company website.

### 4. Business or Corporate Website

A business website provides information about a company, its services, location, contact details, and other offerings. It may also include support resources or a way to request a quote.

### 5. E-commerce Website

An e-commerce website lets people browse products or services and buy them online. Common features include product listings, shopping carts, checkout, and order tracking.

### 6. Educational Website

Educational websites provide learning materials, lessons, courses, exercises, or information about an educational organization. Some offer online classes and track learner progress.

### 7. News and Magazine Website

These websites publish news reports, features, reviews, or other articles. Content may be updated frequently and organized by topic.

### 8. Entertainment and Media Website

Entertainment websites provide or promote media such as music, films, games, podcasts, or videos. Some let users stream or interact with content.

### 9. Social Networking and Community Website

These websites help people communicate, share content, form groups, or discuss topics. They may allow users to create profiles and post comments or messages.

### 10. Government and Nonprofit Website

Government websites provide public information and services. Nonprofit websites explain an organization's mission, programs, events, and ways to volunteer or donate.

### 11. Portal or Directory Website

A portal gathers links, information, or services in one place. A directory organizes listings, such as businesses or services, so users can browse or search them.

### 12. Web Application

A web application is a website that provides interactive functions or services, such as editing documents, managing finances, booking appointments, or collaborating with a team. Many web applications require users to sign in.

## Types of Websites by How They Are Built

### Static Website

A static website serves pages that are generally prepared in advance and remain the same for visitors until someone updates them. It can be simple, fast, and inexpensive to host.

### Dynamic Website

A dynamic website can create or change content in response to data, user actions, or other conditions. It may use server-side software and a database. Examples include online stores, social networks, and sites with personalized accounts.

Many modern sites combine static pages with dynamic features, so the distinction is not always absolute.

## Website vs. Webpage vs. Web Application

- **Website:** A group of related pages and resources under a site identity or domain.
- **Webpage:** One page or document within a website.
- **Web application:** A website or web-based service that lets users perform interactive tasks. The terms can overlap, because web applications are accessed through websites.

## Basic Parts of a Website

- **Domain name:** The human-readable name people use to find the site.
- **Web pages and content:** Text, images, video, files, and interactive elements.
- **Web server or hosting service:** Makes the site available to visitors.
- **Navigation:** Links and menus that help visitors move between pages.
- **Design and layout:** How information is arranged and presented.
- **Security and accessibility:** Practices and features that help protect the site and make it usable by a wide range of people.

## Conclusion

A website is a group of related pages and resources that people access through a browser. Websites serve many purposes, including sharing personal work, publishing information, teaching, selling products, and providing interactive services. They may also be described as static or dynamic based on how their content is produced and updated.
